Inquisitive

Twelve Things I Keep Going Back To

LujonHenry Mancini
Full track →

Most people are taught it as a childhood trait, something you have plenty of at six and are expected to grow out of by twenty, as though the questions were a phase rather than the point. I have come to think it is closer to a discipline, and an unfashionable one, because it costs you something socially to keep asking after the conversation has agreed to stop. What follows is not everything I find interesting. It is the twelve I keep going back to, and what I have noticed is that they are really only three questions wearing different clothes.

How people are persuaded

  1. [ 01 ]Creative writingHow a sentence gets somebody to feel something they did not arrive intending to feel.
  2. [ 02 ]Comedy & timingThe same question with a stopwatch on it. Why one beat lands and the identical line half a second later does not.
  3. [ 03 ]Interface designPersuasion with no words in it at all, which is the hardest version.
  4. [ 04 ]MandarinWhat I can and cannot say to my mother in her own language, and what that gap costs both of us.

How value hides

  1. [ 05 ]Finance & valuationWhat a business is quietly worth to somebody who understands it better than its accounts do.
  2. [ 06 ]Consumer M&AWhy two companies on the same shelf clear seven times apart.
  3. [ 07 ]Education & attentionThe most valuable thing a society allocates, handed out by postcode.
  4. [ 08 ]InequalityNot the fact of it. The machinery that keeps it standing after everyone agrees it should not.

How a body holds up

  1. [ 09 ]Physiology & recoveryMechanism first, always. Not that sleep matters but what glymphatic clearance actually is.
  2. [ 10 ]Self-quantificationRunning bloodwork against my own baselines, which sounds clinical and is really curiosity pointed inward.
  3. [ 11 ]Decision making under stressWhat happens to judgement at mile nine, and whether any of it can be trained.
  4. [ 12 ]EnduranceThe only laboratory I have where the variable is me.
