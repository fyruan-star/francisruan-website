Stewardship

Keeper of the House

IvyFrank Ocean
Full track →

I want the second word. Not because the first is wrong, but because the first describes where you stand and the second describes what you are responsible for, and I have found the second more useful.

A steward is the roommate who washes the dishes. Who plans the trip without announcing that he is planning it, books the place, works out who is free on which weekend, and follows up with the two people who never replied.

What I am after is narrower than doing those things. It is doing them without keeping score. I have known people who do all of it and resent some of it, and the resentment is the part that fails, because it arrives eventually and it arrives at the worst moment. The version I want is the one where the ledger never opens.

If the house is kept and nobody noticed who kept it, that is the whole result.

Three places have taught me most of what I know about this.

Sports

Everyone on that football field was bigger than me. Everyone, including the kicker, and I did check. Freshman year I had never played a down. I was one of very few Asian kids in the programme and I was the worst player out there, which my friends confirmed at the time and have confirmed since.

That hurt for about two years. Not dramatically. In the small daily way, at practice, a little bit every afternoon.

Then I stopped watching the ball. I cannot tell you the day it happened. A player on the field sees his assignment and the man across from him and the next two seconds, which is the correct thing for him to be looking at. Somebody has to watch the whole thing, and that somebody is usually whoever has the time. I had the time.

Four years of football, three on varsity, and a stretch as captain. I ran track. I play lacrosse at USC now and serve as the alumni chair. The useful part was never the playing. It was learning that a team has a second job running underneath the first one, and that nobody is assigned to it.

Student government

I was student body president for four terms and gave the commencement speech. Those are the two lines that go on a page, and they are the least representative part of the work.

What the job actually was: finding out which room was free, asking the teacher who controls that room, remembering that the custodial staff have to be told before you move two hundred chairs, chasing the sign-up sheet, and standing somewhere unglamorous at seven in the morning so that something could begin at eight.

An election gives you a title. It does not give you anything else. The authority comes later and it comes from people deciding, slowly and on their own, that when you say a thing will be handled it is handled. I have not found a shortcut to that and I have stopped looking.

Relationships

My mother gave me a set of instructions and did not present them as optional. Buy the flowers, and buy them when there is no occasion, because an occasion is only a permission slip. Say the compliment out loud instead of thinking it warmly and assuming it arrived. Never go to bed upset, however late that makes the night. Open the door, every time, for the rest of your life.

I send one person a real compliment every day. Specific, unprompted, to somebody who was not expecting anything from me that morning. It takes about eleven seconds and I have never regretted sending one.

The harder instruction is the one about going wrong. When something has broken between two people, sit down and work out honestly how much of it is yours. It usually is, partly. It is almost never only yours, which is the entire reason the word is we. Take the accountability past the point where taking it still makes you feel good about yourself, and then go a little further.

I have loved people who did not love me back in the same way. Some people read that as a weakness and they may be right. I would still rather be the one who went first.

If you want somebody to handle the logistics, I am genuinely the person for it. fyruan@usc.edu
